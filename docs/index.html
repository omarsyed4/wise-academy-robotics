How the mock interview works

  • The interviewer reads a question. The answerer gets one minute, with no notes and no slides.
  • Mark every answer: ✓ said it clearly, ~ got there with help, ✗ couldn't answer.
  • The interviewer always asks one follow-up, “Why?” or “Can you show me?”, because judges always do.
  • Swap roles every 10 minutes. Every ✗ gets fixed on the board or the slides, then asked again before 10:50.
Robot Design questionsMahdi and Ammar ask these
  1. Which missions did you choose, and why those?
  2. Which missions did you skip, and why?
  3. How did you decide the order?
  4. Show me one attachment. What does it do, and who built it?
  5. How does the robot know where it is? Do you use any sensors?
  6. Tell me about something that failed. What did you change?
  7. How many times have you tested your best mission? How often does it work?
  8. How do you line the robot up the same way every time?
  9. How does the robot get from the Blue side to the Red side?
  10. Who did what on the robot? Everyone answers for themselves.
  11. What would you improve with more time?
  12. What did you learn from building this robot?

COACH CALL

Show how we learned

The judges want to hear the students' process: what we tried, what happened, what we changed and what we learned. A clear drawing and an honest explanation can be useful even while the project is unfinished.

Own the explanation

Use Identify → Design → Create → Iterate → Communicate as prompts. Practise answering from the rubric, beyond memorising a script. Students handle questions; coaches and observing parents stay silent.

Make it work on paper

Bring readable drawings, code screenshots and a brief evidence pack. No venue TV or power is promised. Digital material is optional, offline and charged; practise the same explanation with it switched off.

Name the six values

Discovery, Innovation, Impact, Inclusion, Teamwork and Fun. Each student should be ready with a real team example. Support one another while presenting; the behaviour matters too.

Turn setbacks into evidence

M8's rightward miss and alignment changes already tell a useful story. Keep the failed version, the change and the retest result. Record team/expert conversations as: who, when, advice, what we changed, result.

Practise a legal match

No hand transfers between homes, no switching sides and no hand-resetting field models during a match. Same-side technician swaps must keep at most two at a home. Only students handle robots and programming; leave computers/tablets in the pit.

Check the field

Choose and verify the M13–15 model/dock layout before the start. The call described a queue selection sheet; confirm the procedure with our event. Check no-equipment mission conditions before trying detached mechanisms.

03

All fifteen missions

Click a mission to flip it. Seven active missions plus untouched M10 are in the plan; M15 has been explored. The conditional plan ceiling is 290. It is not a demonstrated match score.

0130

Drone Survey

Drone off the mat 20, LiDAR map flipped with the scan marker in the survey area plus 10.

RedCoded

Red rates this easy, though the bonus is the harder half. Back in September, Elias and Ali both wrote it off as low value and hard, yet it stayed in the plan.

CODE
0230

Exploding Seeds

10 for each of three seeds no longer touching the stalk.

Not in plan

Rated "mid value, relatively easy, 30 points for not much work" by both Elias and Ali. Nobody has tried it.

0330

Flip the Rock

Research flag down 20, rock returned to its starting position plus 10.

RedCoded

Red rates this hard. Measured at 4 to 5 seconds, the fastest timing we have.

CODE
0430

Lucky Leaves

First leaf off the nest 10, second leaf off with the katydid still in place plus 20.

Not in plan

The only mission that can score zero after you do the work. The leaves are randomised and you are not told where the katydid starts. If it ends up completely outside the leaf habitat, the whole mission scores nothing. Late season at the earliest.

0520

Reaching Roots

Partially extended 10, or completely extended 20.

RedCoded

There is no bonus here. The Red game plan has it as 10 plus a 10 bonus. It scores 10 or 20, never both. Aiming for a bonus that does not exist means building the wrong attachment.

CODE
0640

Leafcutter Frenzy

Ant touching the nest, then 10 for each of four leaf fragments inside it.

BlueCoded

Highest value mission on the plan, with recorded timings of 7.10s and 8.99s. Every student rated it top tier.

CODE
0740

Humongous Fungus

Mycelium fully extended 20, plus 10 for each connection with the opposing team's extended root.

Not in plan

Only 20 of this is in our control. The other 20 depends on the opposing team fully extending their root, so treat it as a 20 point mission with upside.

0830

Tangled

Vine touching the mat. One action.

RedCoded

Best points per line of code on the whole board. Ali coded it on 9 October and it runs fourth for Red. Four logged runs: too many rotations, then too far right, then two that worked once he moved it left. Timed at 7.95s and 8.78s.

CODE
0930

Research Platform

Platform raised 10, camera trap deployed 10, seed off the tree 10.

Best unclaimed

Thirty points in one location. Three separate conditions clustered together, so even two of the three is worth the trip. Elias and Ali both called it "solid value, multiple bonuses." The strongest mission we are not attempting.

1020

Fragile Microhabitats

Spider and snail habitats left in their starting positions, 10 each.

Free

No code and no launch. Twenty points for not hitting anything, which means every other route has to stay clear of them. Three students wrote this down as 30 points. It is 20.

1120

Window to the Past

Root cover down, touching the mat.

BlueCoded

Rated 3 out of 10, the easiest on the plan, and first in the Blue run order. Ali and Elias both logged it as Mission 7 by mistake, which is Humongous Fungus.

CODE
1230

Forest Elder

Cane raised touching the tree 20, support tie around the post 10.

BlueCoded

Rated 8 out of 10, the hardest on the plan. The Blue game plan logs this as 20 points. It is 30. The support tie is a separate scoring line, not a bonus, so they are writing off 10 points they could have.

CODE
1330

Keystone Species

Our own keystone species model on the restoration platform, young trees raised.

Not in plan

We build the model ourselves from bag 20. It counts as equipment and goes through pre match inspection, so the referee will ask us to identify it.

1440

Seeds of Renewal

5 for each of four seeds in the replantation station, plus 5 each if also touching the mat.

Not in plan

Forty points but spread across eight separate conditions, so partial credit comes easily. Mohamed and Ammar both logged it as 30.

1540

Biocentric Architecture

Canopy raised 10, skylight in 10, compost hatch open 10, plus a 10 environmental bonus.

BlueTried 9 Oct

Blue tried it on 9 October; the window part was not working in that record. They tried making the robot turn, and it didn't work. Not in the plan or the run order as of 9 October.

Do all three actions and the dock position stops mattering, because the bonus scores from whichever dock the model sits on. Mohamed and Ammar both logged this as 60 points. It is 40.

PRE20

Equipment inspection

Robot and all equipment inside one launch area, under 12 inches.

Drill

Worth the same as Window to the Past and takes thirty seconds to practise. The pack down should be identical every single time.

ALL50

Precision tokens

Six at the start. Six or five left both pay 50.

Drill

The first interruption outside home still loses a token; five remaining retains 50 points. The second costs 15, a substantial loss against a 20-point mission. After that: 4 tokens is 35, 3 is 25, 2 is 15, 1 is 10. Wait the extra two seconds for the robot to come home.
