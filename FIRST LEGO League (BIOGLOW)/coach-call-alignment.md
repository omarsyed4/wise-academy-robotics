## Overall assessment

Keep the two judged-area structure and the Identify → Design → Create → Iterate → Communicate sequence. It is consistent with the judging materials and the call. Change the readiness threshold and presentation emphasis: students need to explain their actual work, including unfinished ideas, failures and learning. A polished board is a supporting artifact, not a substitute for understanding or a condition for attending a scrimmage.

The biggest unresolved question is **what specific solution to deforestation the students are proposing**. A topic, broad beneficiaries and three research links are progress, but they do not yet show a mechanism, what is new, or how feedback improved it. That evidence gap should lead the next work, rather than filling every decorative board box.

## Insights we did not previously make explicit

1. **The test log is already presentation material.** Four M8 attempts, with two successes after alignment changes, are a more useful engineering story than a blanket claim that seven missions are finished and easy. The seven logged attempts (Ali four, Elias three) are not seven full matches or seven successful missions.
2. **The real bottleneck is the evidence handoff.** Captions were written without confirmed photo uploads; two Canva links existed without a selected current version; the Robot Design worksheet was recorded empty. Track submitted, accessible and verified separately. A checkmark should never silently mean all three.
3. **A drawing can unblock both conversation and development.** The call's pencil-sketch example means a clear labelled sketch is a practical next step. It lets students explain the proposed mechanism and ask for feedback before spending more time polishing Canva.
4. **The split into board builders and field workers needs shared understanding.** Production roles are useful, but each child needs a real contribution and enough understanding to discuss it. Do not count their name or photo on a board as evidence of building/coding competence.
5. **Match readiness needs a combined run.** Blue's 90 plus Red's 110 are conditional mission values. Adding M10, inspection and tokens yields a 290-point ceiling, not a proven score. Both squads share the same 150 seconds, including returns, attachment changes and handoffs; M12's support tie is still unverified.
6. **Backups are a student engineering responsibility.** Native program exports, dated versions, a second copy/device and a successful restore test address the existing laptop risk. Screenshots and printouts support judging but cannot restore the robot program. Practise recovering from a missing speaker or failed device too.
7. **New official guidance matters now:** the 14 October updates add variable tree stabilization for M8/M9 and permit autonomous use of the interchangeable models' built-in dock latch. Test both taped and untaped tree conditions. This is new official evidence beyond the supplied call, not something attributed to its speakers.
